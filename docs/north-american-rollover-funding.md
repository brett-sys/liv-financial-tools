# How Rollover & Transfer Funds Reach North American — By Check From Most Institutions

**LIV Financial — internal reference sheet**
*Prepared for agent/client conversations about how transferred money arrives at the carrier.*

---

## The short version

When a client moves qualified money (IRA/401(k) rollover) or does a **1035
exchange** into a North American annuity, **the relinquishing (current)
institution most commonly sends the funds as a physical check** — not a wire or
ACH. The check is mailed to North American and clears there before the new
contract is funded. This is normal and expected; a check in the mail does **not**
mean anything went wrong.

## Why it comes by check

Most banks, custodians, and insurance carriers still fund outgoing transfers and
1035 exchanges by issuing a paper check. Wire/ACH is available at some
institutions but is the exception, not the rule. Because each relinquishing
institution controls how it sends money, **check is the lowest-common-denominator
method that virtually every institution supports** — which is why "it'll come by
check" is the safe default answer to give a client.

## The check is payable to the carrier — not to the client

This is the part that protects the client's tax treatment:

- On a **1035 exchange** or a **direct/trustee-to-trustee rollover**, the check is
  made payable to **North American Company FBO [Client Name]** ("FBO" = *for the
  benefit of*) and mailed to North American.
- The client should **never** receive a check made payable to *them personally*
  for a 1035 exchange or a direct rollover. If they do, the IRS can treat it as a
  taxable surrender/distribution rather than a tax-free exchange or direct
  rollover.
- So "the money comes by check" is true — but it's a **carrier-payable check**,
  which keeps the transfer direct and tax-free.

## Typical timeline

A check-based transfer generally takes **~2–6 weeks** end to end (relinquishing
institution processing + mail time + North American posting the funds).

## Source / authoritative document to show a client

North American publishes the transfer paperwork that reflects this process. The
industry-standard form for this is the **1035 Exchange / Rollover / Transfer form
(ACORD 951 family)**, and North American's own transfer form directs the current
institution to send funds to North American on the client's behalf:

- **North American Company — Transfer Form (official):**
  https://www.northamericancompany.com/documents/35457/442918/TransferForm.pdf/bc1da408-1a41-4d19-80ae-2f3daf34c7d1?version=1.0
- **1035 Exchange / Rollover / Transfer form (ACORD 951, industry standard):**
  https://www.goshopinsurance.com/pdf/951.pdf

> For the definitive, current version of any form, always pull it from North
> American's agent portal / forms library rather than a cached copy, and confirm
> payee/mailing instructions on the specific form the client signs.
